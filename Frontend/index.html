    04 — Field guidance

    Prevention is the first early warning.

    Simple, evidence-based practice stops transmission before a cluster forms. These guidelines synthesise WHO recommendations and Indian public-health advisories.

    SafeSip can surface this guidance contextually when a locality’s risk score rises, turning an alert into an immediate, practical next step.

    01

    Clean water practices

    • Boil water for at least 3 minutes before drinking.
    • Use WHO-recommended filtration or chlorination where boiling is not possible.
    • Store drinking water in covered, clean containers with narrow mouths.
    • Never dip hands or used cups directly into storage containers.

    Source · WHO WASH guidelines

    02

    Hygiene & sanitation

    • Wash hands with soap for at least 20 seconds before eating and after using the toilet.
    • Keep toilets and surroundings clean and dry to discourage flies.
    • Dispose of children’s faeces safely — never in open drains or near water sources.
    • Discourage open defecation through community-led campaigns.

    Aligned with Swachh Bharat Mission

    03

    When to seek medical help

    • Persistent diarrhea — more than 3 loose stools in 24 hours.
    • Signs of dehydration: extreme thirst, infrequent urination, dizziness.
    • High fever, blood in stool, or vomiting that does not settle.
    • Infants, pregnant women and older adults should seek care early.

    Consult the nearest PHC or government hospital

    04

    Government & WHO guidance

    • Follow local boil-water advisories and public-health orders.
    • Participate in mass chlorination or vaccination drives when announced.
    • Report suspected outbreaks to ASHA or ANM workers promptly.
    • Use community reporting platforms to shorten the signal-to-response gap.

    Integrates with state health communication

    05 — How it works

    From one report to a coordinated response.

    SafeSip does not replace field teams. It shortens the distance between what a community sees and what a health system can act on.

    01

    Citizens submit symptoms

    ASHA workers and citizens log basic health information, symptoms and water concerns from mobile, kiosk or offline-first tools.

    02

    Water and context collected

    Water-quality sensors stream turbidity, pH and TDS. Field staff add rainfall, repairs and sanitation context.

    03

    Risk engine detects a pattern

    A transparent rule set correlates environmental signals and symptom density to score each area in near real time.

    04

    Responders get a signal

    District officers and PHCs see prioritised hotspots, maps and recommended actions. Demo alerts remain off by default.
